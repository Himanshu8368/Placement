CHAPTER OPENER TEMPLATE  (00_opener.tex)  == the chapter-level master theory
=======================         (DP-series equivalent of theory.txt)
2-3 pages. NO numbered environments here -- they would number as N.0.x.
The opener ORIENTS and ROUTES; it never teaches (that is the topics' job).

STATIONS, in this fixed order
-----------------------------
1. \begin{chaptertheorybox}{Chapter at a Glance}
     - what this chapter is about and why, 3-5 honest sentences;
     - \textbf{You will learn to}  : 4-6 bullets, each a concrete checkable skill
       ("write the -1-sentinel balanced check in one pass", not "understand balance"),
       each bullet ending with the badge \lv{n} of the skill's level;
     - \textbf{Prerequisites.}  \chref/\secref of what is assumed, one sentence each;
     - \textbf{In contests and interviews.}  where this chapter's patterns show up.
   \end{chaptertheorybox}

1b. TWO READING PATHS + ladder position  (levels.txt section 3 -- mandatory)
     \begin{chaptertheorybox}{How to read this chapter}
       two short paragraphs:
       \textbf{Beginner path (\lv1--\lv3):} which stations/proofs/sections to SKIP
         on the first read (name them exactly: "skip the proof of Thm N.k.2, the
         \lv5 entries of Variations in N.3, the Summit");
       \textbf{Full path:} everything in order -- what the reader gets ("up to CF
         \lv6 by the end").
       Then the LADDER STAIRCASE figure: six steps \lv1..\lv6 (colours lv1..lv6),
       this chapter's topics pinned onto the steps they reach -- a TikZ figure,
       see diagrams.txt "ladder staircase" recipe. The reader must see immediately:
       "I am here, the summit is there, these are my steps."
     \end{chaptertheorybox}

2. Roadmap figure  [H], TikZ with the book styles (diagram/flow/boxnode/annot):
     one node per topic + a short result note under each; arrows = dependencies.
     \caption*{...}  (unnumbered -- the opener has no numbered floats).

3. Topic table:  Topic | Key results | Level range | Solved problems
     cells use \secref{...}, \probref{...} -- pointers only, no prose repeats;
     Level range = "$\lv{1}$--$\lv{4}$" style, what the topic climbs to.

4. \subsection*{Problem Recognition Guide --- When You See this, Think that}
   *** the section the DP series made famous; mandatory in every chapter ***
   a) keyword table, 3 columns, one row per topic (rows must map 1:1 onto the
      roadmap nodes -- no row without a topic, no topic without a row):
        \textbf{When the problem says\ldots} & \textbf{Think\ldots} & \textbf{Section}
      Quote the ACTUAL contest phrasing in `` '' (``root-to-leaf'', ``at depth d'',
      ``how many shapes'', ...) and answer with the technique in five words max.
   b) constraint table, 3 columns (Constraint you see | What it permits | Tool):
      turn n and h bounds into algorithm choices; include the recursion-depth row
      whenever the chapter's trees can be skewed and n reaches 10^5+.
   c) \begin{notebox}[Not this chapter?] \end{notebox}
      two or three "if the problem really wants X, you are in the wrong chapter"
      pointers to neighbouring chapters. This box prevents misrouting; keep it short.

   ANTI-REPEAT CONTRACT: the guide routes by PHRASING only. It never states a
   formula, never shows code, never explains why -- the pattern cards (topic.txt)
   own the when/when-not detail, the flowchart (chapter_end.txt) owns the
   post-study revision ladder. The three must not copy each other's wording.

5. \subsection*{Notation Used in This Chapter}  -- 4-column symbol table
   (Symbol | Meaning | Symbol | Meaning), then one conventions paragraph
   (edges-vs-nodes heights, indexing, \nil{} base) if the chapter differs from
   the book default or needs the trap flagged early.

==============================================================================
THEORY-WRITING RULES  (they govern ALL theory prose: opener, topics, solutions)
==============================================================================

ORDER
- Intuition before formalism: a picture of a small tree or a concrete example comes
  before every definition and every theorem.
- Definitions: definition box; the defined word via \term{...} (bold + index entry);
  follow each definition with an example, a non-example, or a picture.
- Formal box first, then friendly unpacking: after each formal box comes plain
  English restatement ("In other words, ...") -- never leave a box untranslated.
- Results: theorem (main results), lemma (steps), corollary (immediate consequences),
  proposition (small facts).

PROOFS  (the book promises in-depth arguments)
- Every result gets a proofbox right after it. Tree facts are usually proved by
  induction on the number of nodes, or on the height, or by a counting/handshaking
  argument; say which.
- A proof states what is assumed and what is to be shown.
- Every step is justified: by the definition, by an earlier numbered result (\thmref ...),
  or by a stated fact. Never write "clearly" or "obviously" for a step that needs an
  argument.
- Name the technique: induction on n, induction on height, contradiction, counting
  (sum of degrees = 2 x edges), extremal choice (a deepest node, a longest path),
  pigeonhole, bijection.
- Long proofs are split into claims ("Claim 1. ...  Proof of Claim 1. ...").
- End with the conclusion in words.
- After a non-obvious proof: an insightbox with its one-sentence idea.
- Show the hypothesis is needed: give a counterexample when it is dropped
  (e.g. "connected" dropped -> a forest; "acyclic" dropped -> a cycle).

ALGORITHMS
- Derive the algorithm from the properties, then state: the recursive structure or
  loop invariant, the base case (usually \nil{} or a leaf), termination, correctness
  (cite the theorem), and complexity (complexitybox).
- Give a hand trace (tracebox or a sequence of small figures) on a small tree.
- Always give BOTH the recursive and the iterative form for traversals where the
  recursion depth can reach n; warn about the call stack.

NUMBERS
- Small, concrete, checkable examples; draw the tree and count on it.
- Tables of small cases (trees with n = 1..8 nodes, all shapes) are encouraged: they
  reveal the pattern (e.g. Catalan numbers) that the theorem then proves.
